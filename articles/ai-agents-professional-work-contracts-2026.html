AI Agents Move into Professional Workflows: OpenAI and Ironclad Research

On October 6, 2026, OpenAI described research with contract-management company Ironclad to train and evaluate AI agents on complex contracting tasks. The work points to a broader shift: AI agents are being tested on complete professional workflows, not just isolated questions.

Published October 10, 2026 · By Ojo Israel · Source: OpenAI

What did the collaboration explore?

OpenAI's research described tasks involving legal, commercial and procurement workflows. Examples included setting up nondisclosure agreements, configuring procurement approval processes and updating reusable legal clauses based on a selected jurisdiction.

These tasks involve more than filling in a form. An agent must understand requirements, configure the right steps, preserve business rules and check whether the completed workflow behaves as intended.

OpenAI reported that, across 11 research tasks, GPT-6 Astra achieved an average rubric score of 55.0%, compared with 41.6% for GPT-5.6 Sol under the tested settings. OpenAI also reported lower estimated time per attempt for Astra. These are results from a limited research evaluation, not a guarantee that an agent can independently handle all real-world legal work.

Read the source: OpenAI: Advancing computer use with Ironclad.

Why this is a meaningful AI trend

Many business tasks require several connected actions and consistent application of rules. A procurement request, for example, may need a spending threshold checked, a security review assigned, finance approval recorded and an agreement stored correctly.

A system that completes each individual click but misses one required approval has not successfully completed the workflow. This is why evaluation needs to check the final outcome and all important constraints, not just whether the agent can operate software.

Where similar ideas could be useful

  • Procurement: Collect requests, check required fields and route approvals based on policy.
  • Customer onboarding: Validate submitted information, create records and track missing documents.
  • Sales operations: Update CRM fields, prepare summaries and assign follow-up tasks based on defined rules.
  • Support operations: Gather information from approved sources, draft responses and escalate exceptions.
  • Internal administration: Turn a request into a checklist of steps, owners and required approvals.

These are potential applications of the underlying approach, not claims that the Ironclad research already delivers every example above as a finished product.

AI agents and workflow automation work best together

In practical automation, an AI model can interpret unstructured information or suggest the next step. A workflow engine such as Make.com or n8n can then apply deterministic rules, call APIs, update records, notify the right person and log the outcome.

For example, an AI-assisted onboarding workflow could read a form and summarize the request. Ordinary rules can check whether required fields are present and determine which team should approve it. The workflow can pause for approval before taking any consequential action.

This division of responsibilities is important. AI is useful for flexible interpretation, while explicit rules are often better for thresholds, required fields, permissions and approval conditions.

Why human oversight still matters

OpenAI's own write-up notes that professional workflows must handle exceptions and preserve business rules, and that human oversight remains important. This is particularly true for contracts, finance, compliance and other high-impact work.

  • Require qualified people to review legal language and consequential decisions.
  • Keep a record of changes and approvals.
  • Test cases above and below thresholds.
  • Make failure states visible instead of silently continuing.
  • Give agents only the access required for their assigned task.

What businesses should do now

Do not begin by giving an AI agent access to every system. Choose a narrow workflow with clear rules, collect realistic test cases, and measure whether the agent completes the whole task accurately. Start with read-only or draft-only access where possible, then add controlled actions only when the evidence supports it.

Measure completion rate, rule violations, review time, failure recovery and cost. A workflow that completes a little less autonomously but is easy to audit may be more valuable than a flashy demo that is hard to trust.

The takeaway

The Ironclad collaboration illustrates an important direction for AI: systems are being trained and evaluated on real professional processes with multiple steps and constraints. Progress is promising, but the reported results also show why careful testing matters.

For automation builders, the opportunity is to connect AI interpretation with reliable integrations, explicit business rules and verification. The aim is not to remove every human decision. It is to reduce repetitive work while keeping important controls in place.

Frequently asked questions

What is computer-use AI?

It describes AI systems that interact with software interfaces to perform tasks, rather than only generating text or code.

Can AI agents manage contracts without humans?

That should not be assumed. Legal work needs qualified review, clear approval controls and verification of the finished result.

How can small businesses apply this trend?

Start with lower-risk workflows such as document intake, CRM updates, request routing and approval reminders, using clear rules and logs.
