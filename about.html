ABOUT · AI · WORKFLOW AUTOMATION

Ojo Israel: AI & Workflow Automation Specialist

I build practical workflows that connect AI, business applications and repeatable processes, helping teams spend less time moving information manually and more time acting on it.

My focus is the practical side of automation: understanding how a process works today, identifying the repetitive or error-prone steps, and designing a workflow that makes those steps easier to manage. That can mean connecting a form to a CRM, using AI to classify an enquiry, routing a support issue to a person, or turning spreadsheet data into a useful report.

This website documents what I learn and build around AI automation, CRM automation, API integrations and business process automation. I aim to explain the decisions behind a workflow, not just list tools or promise that AI will solve everything.

What I help automate

  • Lead capture and follow-up: move enquiries from forms or other sources into a CRM, notify the right person, and trigger timely follow-up with clear stop conditions.
  • CRM and sales operations: keep records in sync, organize lead information, support qualification and reduce repetitive data entry.
  • AI-assisted customer support: classify incoming questions, retrieve relevant knowledge, identify cases that need a human, and log escalations.
  • Document and data processing: extract information from files, structure it into usable records, and add checks before it moves downstream.
  • Reporting and marketing workflows: collect campaign data, calculate useful metrics, prepare summaries and support content workflows.
  • Application and API integration: connect tools through native integrations, webhooks and APIs when information needs to move between systems.

Selected automation projects

The projects below represent practical builds and demonstrations of workflow design. They show the types of systems I work with; they should not be read as claims of measured client results.

1. AI customer support agent with knowledge retrieval

A workflow built with n8n, Gemini and Supabase that classifies customer messages, uses a knowledge base to support answers, and routes frustrated or sensitive cases for human attention. The design separates knowledge retrieval from the main support workflow and records escalations for follow-up.

2. Lead follow-up and booking workflow

A Make.com workflow connecting a form, Airtable and Google Calendar. It checks availability, creates a booking when a slot is available, sends the relevant email and internal notification, and handles unavailable slots with an alternative next step. Error logging helps make failures easier to investigate.

3. Campaign performance reporting

A Make.com and Google Sheets demonstration that organizes campaign data and calculates metrics such as click-through rate, cost per click, conversion rate and cost per conversion before generating a plain-language summary with AI.

4. Invoice information extraction

A document-processing workflow that takes a file from Google Drive, extracts invoice details with document and AI tools, and sends structured values to Google Sheets for review and use.

5. AI knowledge base and retrieval-augmented generation

A retrieval workflow using n8n, Gemini embeddings and Supabase Vector Store to make stored reference material searchable by meaning. This kind of system needs attention to source quality, retrieval relevance and what the assistant should do when the answer is not supported by the available information.

Tools and technologies

I work with visual workflow platforms and connected business tools, including:

  • Automation: Make.com and n8n
  • AI: large language models and AI APIs, including Gemini and OpenAI-based workflows
  • CRM and data: HubSpot, Airtable, Google Sheets and Supabase
  • Integrations: webhooks, REST APIs, structured data and Google Workspace

The tool choice should follow the process, budget, reliability needs and existing systems. A workflow does not need AI at every step; predictable rules are often better for decisions that must be consistent.

How I approach automation

  1. Understand the process. Identify the trigger, inputs, decisions, handoffs and desired outcome.
  2. Keep the workflow understandable. Use clear steps, structured data and sensible naming so it can be maintained.
  3. Validate important information. Do not blindly trust AI output or pass incomplete data into business-critical actions.
  4. Plan for exceptions. Include retries or error logging where appropriate, plus human review for uncertain or sensitive cases.
  5. Test before relying on it. Check normal cases, missing fields, duplicate events, unavailable services and failure paths.
